You opted out of a people-search site, saved the confirmation email, and searched your name a week later. Your home address was right back on Google. Before you fill out the same form again, open the exact result. A stale snippet, a live broker profile, and an old PDF can all put the same address on your screen. Each calls for a different request.

## Open the result before you file a request

Start with your name and city. Copy the URL behind the search result and open it in a signed-out browser window to see what a visitor can see. If it opens a PDF, check the document itself. Record each URL separately.

| What you find | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| The live page or PDF still shows your address | Ask the publisher to remove the address or use the broker’s official opt-out. You can also ask Google to remove the eligible result. |
| Google still shows the address from a removed or updated page | Submit that URL through Google’s Refresh Outdated Content tool. |
| The address appears at a different URL | Check who runs that page and follow its removal process. Keep a separate entry for it. |

### When the address still appears on the live page

In Google Search, open the three-dot menu next to the result and choose **Remove result** if the option is available. Follow the prompts for personal contact information. Google’s private-information form also accepts requests for eligible home addresses. Google reviews the submitted URLs and can keep results it considers valuable to the public. Check the publisher’s page separately, since a Google approval applies to Search. [Results about you](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/12719076?hl=en) · [Google’s private-information removal form and policy](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/9673730?hl=en)

### When only the old Google result remains

Submit the exact URL to Google’s **Refresh Outdated Content** tool. If the tool asks which text changed, enter a word or two that still appear in the snippet and have disappeared from the live page. Google can remove the result for a deleted page or clear the old snippet until it crawls the changed page again. [Refresh Outdated Content instructions](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7041154?hl=en)

## Why your address can come back after a data broker opt-out

People-search sites can assemble profiles from public records, other brokers, and public social profiles. Your address can still appear in someone else’s report, and changes to public records can bring your information back into a people-search listing. If the old profile comes back at the same URL, follow up with the original request ID and the date you last saw it gone. If you find another listing, start by checking the broker’s opt-out instructions for that URL. [FTC guide to opting out of people-search sites](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-people-search-sites-sell-your-information)

An odd detail can give you a lead. Suppose two listings use the same misspelling of your street, and you recognize it from a club’s archived meeting minutes. Check the public PDF. If it still includes your home address, you have a place to ask for a correction, even if you can’t tell where either broker got the information.

For a PDF, give the publisher the direct file URL and page number. Ask for a replacement with the address redacted from the underlying text and any scanned image. Check both the visible page and searchable text in the replacement. If the publisher uploads it at a new URL, check the old URL too. For an official public record, ask the office that maintains it what redaction or address-substitution options it offers. [Adobe’s guide to PDF redaction](https://helpx.adobe.com/acrobat/desktop/protect-documents/redact-pdfs/redacting-sanitizing.html)

**A short request you can adapt**

> Please redact my home address from \[URL\], on \[page or section\]. Could you confirm when the public copy has been updated? If your office uses a specific form for this request, please send me the link.

## Keep a record of what actually disappeared

Keep one entry per URL in a private note. When you get a confirmation, check the live URL again and run the search that found it. Record the two observations separately. If a request is still pending, set the next check around the stated response window for that site. If you see the same address later, your dates tell you whether the original listing resurfaced or whether you’re looking at a copy you hadn’t found.

```sql
URL
Date found
Request date and confirmation ID
Date checked and address visible at source?
Date checked and address visible in Google?
Next check date
```
