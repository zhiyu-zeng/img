Confidence is high. You're on an iOS mobile device. It’s a trusted website that you visit every day to check the headlines. Just a routine visit. But once you’ve browsed away, you’ve been unknowingly infected with nation-state-level malware installed on that same ‘safe’ device. You never even clicked on anything. Not just passwords, but MFA tokens and browsing sessions are fully accessible by anyone willing to pay, and the persistence continues without even a whisper that it’s there.

The recent attack on 178 of the world’s most popular and trusted sites: Salesforce, Zoom, CNN, Fox News, the BBC, et al came and went from the news cycle surprisingly fast given the scale of the attack and the brands involved. There was a brief discussion of crypto theft and banking passwords, but it was treated as just another breach. It was not.

Researchers identified malicious code delivered through third-party website services that directed vulnerable iOS devices toward exploit kits, including **Coruna** and **DarkSword**. This is an active exploit delivery through legitimate websites, not a hypothetical attack scenario. An affected page could appear completely normal while malicious loaders run in the background without so much as a click required by the user.

As we sit here more than a week later, the large majority of security organizations have absolutely no way to determine if an infection took place (and is still persistent) on an employee device. A device that for most organizations is at the heart of identity and authentication to their most confidential business systems and data. Zero. Visibility.
