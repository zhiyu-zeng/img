Ransomware reached record levels in Q3 2026, but the scale of activity is only part of what changed. The ecosystem producing those attacks is becoming increasingly fragmented: more ransomware brands are claiming victims, the top groups account for a smaller share of overall activity, and several major organizations are pushing back against high-value ransom demands. At the same time, established operators such as Qilin and The Gentlemen remain firmly at the top, creating a landscape where strong leaders coexist with an increasingly crowded field of ransomware groups.

Key observed findings

-   Q3 2026 was the highest-volume quarter on record, with 2,830 victims published on data leak sites. July recorded 964 victims, August reached 1,042, and September recorded 824.
-   The ransomware ecosystem became more fragmented. The top 10 groups’ share of victims fell for the third consecutive quarter, from 71% in Q1 to 58% in Q2 and 49% in Q3. For the first time, groups outside the top ten collectively accounted for the majority of observed victims.
-   The number of active ransomware brands continued to grow. 117 distinct brands claimed at least one victim in Q3, up from 91 in Q2. Of these, 51 had not claimed a victim in Q2, and 42 made their first observed victim claims during Q3.
-   Qilin remained on top with The Gentlemen still close by. Following the ending of Q2, when for the first time in 2026 Qilin briefly relinquished the leading position in favor of The Gentlemen for one month, Qilin reclaimed first place in July and August, before being overtaken yet again by The Gentlemen in September. Eventually Qilin remained in top position for yet another quarter by a narrow gap.
-   LockBit’s decline continued throughout 2026. Once one of the most prolific ransomware operations, LockBit recorded fewer victims in each successive quarter and fell out of the top ten altogether in Q3.
-   Several major organizations rejected high-value extortion demands. Stadler Rail, the Berlin state government and Manchester Airports Group were among the high-profile organizations that publicly refused ransom demands during the quarter.
-   Ransomware payment patterns continued to diverge. Average payments fell 45% quarter over quarter to just over $1 million, while the median increased 167% to $400,000, pointing to different dynamics between routine payments and the largest extortion demands.
-   The ShinyHunters-Clop dispute exposed rivalry within the ransomware ecosystem. ShinyHunters reportedly breached and defaced Clop’s leak site and demanded a public apology alongside payment, while Q3 also brought increased law-enforcement pressure against cybercriminal operations.
