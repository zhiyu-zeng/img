You find your address on a people-search site, hit the removal link, and get a box asking for a picture of your driver’s licence. Pause there. Find out which request you’ve opened and what the site needs to identify your listing.

Start from the site’s own privacy page and follow its link to the form. If a removal email sent you somewhere else, use the contact information the broker publishes on its privacy page to check that the email belongs to your case. An unexpected attachment request can wait while you check the sender. [FTC guide to finding a broker’s opt-out process](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-people-search-sites-sell-your-information)

## Look at what the form says it will do

A broker might offer to hide a public listing, stop selling or sharing the information, or delete records it holds. Figure out which action you want and look for that option by name. If the visible listing is your immediate concern, have its URL handy. It’s easier for a broker to match the right profile when it knows which page you mean.

California makes one distinction especially clear. For a business covered by the CCPA, a request to opt out of sale or sharing can ask for information needed to carry it out. The business cannot require identity verification for that request. A request to delete data can involve identity verification, and California’s privacy regulator says businesses should use the minimum personal information needed for the request. If you’re seeing an ID upload, check which request the form is handling. [CalPrivacy guidance on verification and data minimization](https://privacy.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/357/2026/01/enfadvisory202401-1.pdf)

## Ask whether the broker can verify the listing another way

Start with the listing URL and a detail the broker already associates with you. For example, you might be able to confirm an email address that appears on the record. If the broker still asks for a document, ask which fields it needs and whether you can cover the others. Confirm the answer before you submit anything, especially if a separate company runs the verification page.

You can say it in one reply.

> I’m asking you to remove the listing at \[URL\]. Can you match my request using the information on the listing or an email I control? If you need a document, please tell me which fields you need, how I can submit it securely, and how long you keep it.

If the broker contacts you while a data-removal service is working on your request, match the broker name and request details in your dashboard. The service can tell you which part you need to finish yourself. You may already have a case open for that listing. [Find your requests in Agent Garrett](https://garrettstimpson.ca/posts/2026/10/16/how-to-use-agent-garrett/#3-remove-your-data-from-data-brokers)

When the broker responds, save the response and reopen the original listing. The form and the confirmation tell you how the request was handled. The live page tells you whether someone can still see the address. [Copy a follow-up for a listing that’s still live](https://garrettstimpson.ca/posts/2026/11/17/data-broker-opt-out-request-template/)

California residents handling several registered brokers can also use the state’s own request portal. It verifies eligibility through the state before you submit a deletion request. [How to use California DROP](https://garrettstimpson.ca/posts/2026/11/17/california-drop-data-broker-deletion/)
