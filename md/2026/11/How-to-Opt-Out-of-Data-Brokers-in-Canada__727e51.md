If a people-search site lists your address or phone number in Canada, start with the URL of the listing. Use the site’s opt-out process if it has one. If it asks you to justify the request or says it will keep the information, write to the organization’s privacy contact and ask what it holds, why the listing is public and what it can remove.

## Start with the broker’s privacy contact

Save the exact URL and note which fields are exposed. Use the site’s official privacy or listing removal form. If the site offers one form for a visible listing and another for deletion or sharing preferences, keep a confirmation for each request. For a site outside Canada, its published opt-out can still give you a route to take down the listing. [Copy a data broker opt-out request](https://garrettstimpson.ca/posts/2026/11/17/data-broker-opt-out-request-template/)

## Use the Canadian privacy rules that apply

The Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act, or PIPEDA, generally governs how private-sector organizations handle personal information in commercial activities. Alberta, British Columbia and Quebec have their own private-sector laws that can apply within those provinces. A business handling personal information across provincial or national borders in commercial activities can come under PIPEDA. Use the federal privacy commissioner’s guide to find the rules that fit the organization and activity. [PIPEDA requirements and provincial coverage](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/)

-   **Ask what the organization holds.** Where PIPEDA applies, send a written access request if you need to see what personal information the organization holds and learn how it has been used and disclosed. An organization generally has 30 calendar days to respond, with specific exceptions and extensions. [OPC guide to accessing personal information from a business](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/accessing-personal-information/api_bus/)
-   **Ask for a correction.** If a broker describes your personal mobile as a business number or uses an out-of-date address, identify the incorrect field. Under PIPEDA, you can challenge the accuracy or completeness of your personal information. Ask whether the corrected details can be forwarded to recipients where appropriate. [OPC access and correction guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/accessing-personal-information/api_bus/)
-   **Withdraw consent where it applies.** Ask the organization to stop the uses and sharing that rely on your consent. Withdrawal under PIPEDA is subject to reasonable notice and legal or contractual restrictions. Ask which information it will retain and why. [OPC consent guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/p_principle/principles/p_consent/)

## Follow the address or number back to its source

Suppose a directory copied your personal number from a trades association’s old member list. Ask the directory to correct its listing and ask the association to update the member list. If the source is a government registry, ask the office that maintains it what correction or redaction process it offers. Keep the two URLs and the responses together so you can confirm each change. [How to check where your phone number is still public](https://garrettstimpson.ca/posts/2026/11/17/remove-phone-number-from-internet/)

If an organization leaves the issue unresolved after you contact its privacy officer, check which privacy regulator handles it. Keep the listing URL, your request, the date and the reply for a complaint. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada explains how to raise a concern and when its office can help. [Report a privacy concern](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/report-a-concern/)
