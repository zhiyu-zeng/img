California’s Delete Request and Opt-out Platform, or DROP, lets California residents send one free deletion request to registered data brokers. The portal reports a separate outcome for each broker. Save the confirmation code so you can come back and see which brokers found your records and what happened to them. [Official California DROP portal](https://privacy.ca.gov/drop/)

## Submit your California DROP request

1.  **Verify your residency.** Start at the state’s DROP page. It sends you to the California Identity Gateway to confirm that you qualify. If the check fails and you believe you qualify, use the eligibility review option.
2.  **Enter information that brokers can match.** Follow the form for the required fields. A phone number or email used in an old listing can help a broker find a record associated with it. The form also offers optional fields for identifiers such as mobile advertising IDs and vehicle identification numbers. Choose the relevant details you’re comfortable providing.
3.  **Save the eight-digit DROP ID.** Store it privately with the submission date. You’ll use it to check your status or update the request. The state offers a recovery process if you lose it.

You may be asked for an email address or phone number during both steps. Residency verification checks eligibility. The deletion form uses identifiers to help brokers find your records. [How DROP works](https://privacy.ca.gov/drop/how-drop-works/) · [CalPrivacy’s DROP FAQ](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/10/drop-faq-blog/)

## Read the status for each broker

Brokers started processing requests on August 1, 2026. CalPrivacy says it can take up to 90 days from submission for brokers to report how they handled a request. After initial processing, they must check for and delete new matching non-exempt data at least every 45 days. Your request stays in effect. [CalPrivacy’s timing and status guidance](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/10/drop-faq-blog/)

| Status | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Pending | The broker has yet to report an outcome. Check again during the reporting window. |
| Deleted | The broker matched your request and deleted the personal information it held that was eligible for deletion. |
| Exempted | The broker found a match and reported that an exemption lets it retain the information it holds. |
| Opted-out | The broker could only make a partial match. It may still hold the data and must stop selling or sharing it under this outcome. |
| Record not found | The broker found no matching record using the details in your request. |

For example, a broker might still show a listing tied to an old email address and report **Record not found** for a request that used your current email. Check that the listing belongs to you, then consider adding the older address through your DROP profile. You can update your profile once every 45 days. If you can verify a live listing separately, use the broker’s official privacy channel and give it that exact URL. [Get help with DROP](https://privacy.ca.gov/drop/help-with-drop/) · [Copy a broker opt-out request](https://garrettstimpson.ca/posts/2026/11/17/data-broker-opt-out-request-template/)

## Check any listing you can still see

A DROP status applies to that broker’s records. An address may also appear in the original public record, on another site, or in an older Google result. Check the live URL, find who publishes it, and use that publisher’s process if it still displays your information.
