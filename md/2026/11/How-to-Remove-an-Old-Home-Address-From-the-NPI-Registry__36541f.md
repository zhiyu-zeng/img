The provider page gets your credentials right. Then you see the street address. It’s the apartment you lived in when you filled out the original forms.

Before you email the directory, look up your own National Provider Identifier in the public NPI Registry. Check whether the home address appears under **Mailing Address**, **Primary Practice Address**, or elsewhere in your record. Write down the field and open that same record after you make a change. If the official record already shows a valid business address, you can send the directory a correction with the current CMS listing as evidence. [Public NPI Registry](https://npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov/) · [CMS guidance on what the registry publishes](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/regulations-guidance/administrative-simplification/data-dissemination)

## Correct the address in NPPES first

CMS publishes certain fields from active NPI records in both its registry and downloadable files. For an active NPI, the way to change a public address is to update the underlying record. Sign in to the National Plan and Provider Enumeration System, or have the person authorized to manage the record do it. You can also use the paper NPI Application/Update Form, CMS-10114. [NPPES](https://nppes.cms.hhs.gov/) · [CMS-10114 and instructions](https://www.cms.gov/Medicare/CMS-Forms/CMS-Forms/downloads/cms10114.pdf)

The form asks for two kinds of addresses. The **mailing address** is where you can receive business correspondence about the NPI. The **practice location** is where the primary practice actually operates. A clinic can have both fields set to the same address, and a practice that gets business mail somewhere else may have two different entries. When you work exclusively from home or provide telehealth, check with the NPI Enumerator about what fits your setup before replacing a practice address. [CMS address instructions](https://www.cms.gov/Medicare/CMS-Forms/CMS-Forms/downloads/cms10114.pdf)

If you moved, check both address fields and the practice contact number before saving. CMS says covered health care providers must report an address change within 30 days of the change taking effect. [CMS FAQ on provider moves](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/burden-reduction/administrative-simplification/unique-identifiers/faqs)

## Ask directories to update the copy they have

Reopen the public registry once the change has processed. If a third-party directory still has the old address, give it the URL of its own listing, your NPI, and a link to the current registry record. Ask it to update the specific field that’s wrong. The same approach works if a directory still shows the home number you used before you set up a practice line.

You might find that the directory fixed the page and Google still shows the old apartment in the text below the link. A search refresh is the next step in that case. If the page still displays the address, follow up with whoever publishes that page. [How to check a stale Google result](https://garrettstimpson.ca/posts/2026/11/17/remove-home-address-from-google/)
